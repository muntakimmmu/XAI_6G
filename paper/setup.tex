\section{Testbed, Reproduction and Diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{Vectorised port.} We re-implemented Sentinel's released simulator and
shield~\cite{sentinelcode} in batched NumPy. The link capacity is 1000,
legitimate arrivals are Poisson(300) and an attack of intensity $i$ adds
$1500\,i$. Attack families are SYN, UDP, HTTP, ICMP and mixed; each family has
its own match score per filtering focus, and polymorphic mutation reduces that
score. Observations are aggregate telemetry computed from served traffic. Two
unit tests replay the reference \texttt{DDoSEnv.step} and
\texttt{SymbolicShield.repair} against the port on identical random draws; they
agree within $10^{-5}$ over 400 random steps, including ICMP re-rolls, and over
5{,}000 random shield queries. Evaluating Sentinel's released
benchmark checkpoints (seeds 42--51) with Sentinel's own trained attackers
reproduces its published multi-scenario table. Examples: strong attack
quality/leakage/severe 0.519/0.815/78.2 (published 0.519/0.815/75.0); zero-shot
ICMP 0.529/0.747/0 (published 0.529/0.747/0); chaos 0.701/0.836/22.0
(published 0.699/0.836/25.3). All values are in \texttt{results/DIAGNOSTICS.md}.

\textbf{Diagnostic 1: a physics ceiling.} Overload is computed on the
\emph{pre-filter} load, so a full-intensity flood yields
$\mathrm{SQ}\le 1000/1800=0.556$ whatever the defender does. Every defender
therefore has 500/500 degraded steps under strong attack, and strict SLA
compliance cannot be reached by construction. This is consistent with
Sentinel's reported limitation and is not evidence against its method.

\textbf{Diagnostic 2: the fixed shield caps low-rate mitigation.} We computed
a privileged per-step oracle that knows the attack family, intensity and
mutation and searches a $21\times3\times20$ action grid at every step. Under
Protocol B's mild scenario it reaches a composite of 0.163 without the shield,
but only 0.057 behind the default shield. Sentinel reaches 0.004: its
PanicGuard drop clamp fires on 333 of 500 steps and leakage is 0.99. The
binding constraint in this regime is therefore the \emph{symbolic} layer, which
motivates the shield discovery of Section~\ref{sec:shielddisc}. Under strong
attack the shielded oracle reaches $-0.45$ with no severe outages, while
Sentinel reaches $-0.72$; the remaining gap here is a learning gap.

\textbf{Diagnostic 3: objective and selection mismatches.} The released code
trains the defender on
$\mathrm{SQ}-\mathrm{CD}-\mathrm{leak}-2\,\mathbb{1}[\text{SLA violated}]-0.1\,\mathbb{1}[\text{repaired}]$,
but selects checkpoints with the composite score of Sentinel's Eq.~(15). The
$-2$ SLA term cannot be avoided under overload, and at low rates it rewards
letting the attack through. In addition, the checkpoint-selection routine
includes the zero-shot ICMP scenario in its validation average, so ICMP is
held out from training but not from model selection. NS-CSD trains directly on
the per-step composite (Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}) and validates without ICMP.
We apply the same reward and selection rule to our PPO control, so any
difference between the two is not caused by the objective.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{Methods.} \emph{Sentinel} and \emph{Sentinel-final} are Sentinel's
released benchmark-selected and final (generation 1999) checkpoints for seeds
42--51. \emph{PPO NoShield} is the same benchmark network run without the
shield, as in Sentinel's ablation. The remaining baselines are the
\emph{ShieldOnly} (a random policy behind the shield), \emph{Static},
\emph{Adaptive} and \emph{Random} controllers from Sentinel. \emph{PPO+shield}
is our equal-budget control. It uses the same network with a value head,
GAE, 32 parallel 100-step episodes per update and 420 updates. Its scenario
prior, reward, validation battery and champion selection are those of NS-CSD,
so it removes co-evolution without adding solution discovery.
\emph{NS-CSD} runs 500 iterations with $B{=}32$ contexts, $G{=}8$ members,
horizon $H{=}8$, $W_{\max}{=}40$, a discovery step every $K{=}20$ iterations,
learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, clip 0.2, 4 epochs, $\beta_{\text{KL}}{=}0.02$,
$c_{\text{el}}{=}0.5$ and $c_{\text{H}}{=}0.005$. Both NS-CSD and PPO+shield use
about $1.35$M training environment steps; Sentinel used 200k co-evolution
steps plus validation. The ablations remove one component at a time: shield
discovery, the scenario curriculum, elite injection, the champion anchor with
roll-back, CRN, or the shield altogether. \emph{GRPO+shield} removes all four
discovery mechanisms. \emph{NS-CSD safety-weighted} sets $w_{\text{sev}}{=}2$.
The main methods use 10 seeds (42--51), matching Sentinel. The secondary
ablations use 5 seeds (42--46).

\textbf{Protocols.} \emph{A (Sentinel-native):} Sentinel's own trained
attacker for each seed drives its five scenarios exactly as in its evaluation
script (5 episodes $\times$ 100 steps; leakage averaged over all steps).
NS-CSD never saw these attackers, so this protocol measures transfer.
\emph{B (attacker-agnostic):} open-loop scenarios from $\Xi$: benign, mild,
strong, chaos, zero-shot ICMP, and four held-out stress tests. These are
pulsing floods (period 10, duty 0.5), polymorphic floods (switching
probability 0.5, mutation 0.7--1), boundary-rate floods (intensity 0.3--0.6)
and ICMP-heavy chaos. Each scenario runs 20 episodes of 100 steps, and
leakage is averaged over attacked steps. \emph{C (red team):} for each
defender, a cross-entropy search (48 candidates, 8 rounds) over the full 14-d
scenario space, ICMP included, minimises the composite score; the worst
scenario found is then re-scored on fresh traffic. All defenders see
identical traffic for a given seed and scenario, so comparisons are paired.

\textbf{Statistics.} We report seed-level means with 95\,\% $t$-intervals.
NS-CSD is compared with Sentinel and PPO+shield using paired $t$-tests and
Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, with Holm correction across scenarios for each
metric. Pareto relations use the joint criterion of~\cite{alfatemi2026sentinel}:
quality $\uparrow$, leakage $\downarrow$, severe outages $\downarrow$. Counts are
reported per 500 steps.
